06 · BOOLEAN ALGEBRA

Boolean Algebra — simplifying digital logic

Digital circuits operate with two logical states, 0 and 1. Boolean algebra is the mathematics used to describe and manipulate these states. AND is written as ·, OR as +, and NOT as a complement such as A̅. A circuit can therefore be represented by an equation, simplified mathematically, and then implemented using fewer or simpler gates.

0 / 1 inputs→Boolean expression→Boolean laws→Simplified expression→Simpler circuit

Why Boolean laws?

A Boolean expression may contain repeated or unnecessary terms. The laws allow an expression to be replaced by an equivalent simpler expression without changing its truth table.

How does this help hardware?

Reducing literals and operations can reduce gates, gate inputs and interconnections. The function remains the same, but the circuit becomes easier to implement and understand.

Why prove the laws?

A law must hold for every possible input. A truth table calculates LHS and RHS independently. If the final columns are identical for all rows, the identity is proved.

How does this connect to K-maps?

Boolean algebra simplifies symbolically. A K-map performs the same type of simplification visually by grouping adjacent combinations that differ in only one variable.

Algebra and K-map are doing the same simplification

A̅B̅ + A̅B = A̅(B̅+B) = A̅

Only B changes between the two terms. Algebraically, B̅+B=1, so B disappears. On a K-map, the corresponding adjacent cells are grouped and the changing variable B disappears from the simplified term.

Proof method used below: list all possible inputs → calculate LHS → calculate RHS → compare. If LHS = RHS in every row, the law is proved.
01 · BASIC LAWS

One-variable Boolean laws

Identity Law

LHS = RHS proof
A+0=A    |    A·1=A

Meaning: OR with 0 cannot add a 1, and AND with 1 cannot force the output to 0. Therefore A passes through unchanged.

Annulment / Null Law

LHS = RHS proof
A+1=1    |    A·0=0

Meaning: one 1 is enough to make an OR output 1; one 0 is enough to make an AND output 0. The constant therefore dominates the operation.

Idempotent Law

LHS = RHS proof
A+A=A    |    A·A=A

Meaning: repeating the same signal does not create new information. Two copies of A connected to OR or AND still produce A.

Complement Law

LHS = RHS proof
A+A̅=1    |    A·A̅=0

Meaning: A and A̅ always have opposite values. At least one is always 1, so their OR is 1; they can never both be 1, so their AND is 0. This law appears very frequently during simplification and K-map reasoning.

02 · TWO-VARIABLE LAWS

Order, grouping and redundancy

Commutative Law

A+B=B+A    |    AB=BA

Meaning: swapping the inputs of an AND or OR gate does not change its output.

Absorption Law

A+AB=A    |    A(A+B)=A

Why “absorption”? In A+AB, whenever AB is 1, A is already 1. Therefore AB cannot add any new case to the output and is absorbed by A. This is particularly useful for removing redundant terms.

04 · PRINCIPLE OF DUALITY

Many Boolean laws come in pairs

The Principle of Duality says that a valid Boolean identity remains valid when we simultaneously interchange:

+ ↔ ·and0 ↔ 1

The variables and their complements are left unchanged. Duality is not the same as taking the complement of an expression.

OriginalDual
A+0=AA·1=A
A+1=1A·0=0
A+A=AA·A=A
A+A̅=1A·A̅=0
A+BC=(A+B)(A+C)A(B+C)=AB+AC
Why useful? Once one member of a dual pair is understood, the other is easier to remember. In an exam, when asked to “find the dual,” change + to ·, · to +, 0 to 1 and 1 to 0. Do not complement the variables.
05 · DE MORGAN'S THEOREMS

How to move a complement through an expression

De Morgan’s theorems are especially important because they tell us how to complement a complete AND/OR expression. They also explain the relationship between NAND/NOR implementations and AND/OR logic.

(A+B)̅ = A̅·B̅

The complement of an OR is the AND of the individual complements.

(AB)̅ = A̅+B̅

The complement of an AND is the OR of the individual complements.

Memory rule: BREAK THE BAR → CHANGE THE OPERATOR → COMPLEMENT EACH VARIABLE

Example 1

(A+B+C)̅=A̅B̅C̅

The OR signs become AND, and every variable is complemented.

Example 2

(ABC)̅=A̅+B̅+C̅

The AND operation becomes OR, and every variable is complemented.

07 · EXAM PRACTICE

What should be prepared?

1. State the laws

Be able to write Identity, Null/Annulment, Idempotent, Complement, Double Negation, Commutative, Associative, Distributive, Absorption and De Morgan’s theorems correctly.

2. Prove a law using a truth table

Write all input combinations, calculate intermediate columns if needed, calculate LHS and RHS separately, and conclude: “Since LHS = RHS for all possible input combinations, the law is proved.”

3. Simplify using Boolean laws

Show each transformation and mention the law used. Typical forms include A+AB, A(A+B), AB+A B̅, and expressions requiring distributive and complement laws.

4. Apply De Morgan’s theorem

Be able to find complements such as:

F=A+BC   ⇒   F̅=A̅(B̅+C̅)

5. Find the dual

Interchange AND ↔ OR and 0 ↔ 1. Remember: finding a dual is not the same as complementing the expression.

6. Connect expression to circuit

Recognize that each product term corresponds to an AND operation, sums correspond to OR operations, and complements require inversion. A simplified Boolean expression therefore leads directly to a simplified gate network.

Quick revision order: know the law → understand what it means → verify it by LHS/RHS truth table → use it in simplification → connect the simplified expression to the logic circuit and K-map.
