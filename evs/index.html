01

What is an ecosystem made of?

An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with one another and with the physical environment. Its structure comes from the arrangement of biotic and abiotic components.

☀️Sunlightabiotic
🌱Producerplant/algae
🐇Consumerherbivore
🦊Consumercarnivore
🍄Decomposerfungi/bacteria

Structure = who is present + where + how they interact

🌿🐾🍄

Biotic components

Producers capture energy; consumers obtain energy by feeding; decomposers break down dead organic matter and return nutrients.

☀️💧🪨🌬️

Abiotic components

Sunlight, water, air, soil and minerals influence where organisms live and how abundant they can become.

Spatial + temporal structure

Structure also changes across space and time: plant layers, soil composition, seasons, disturbances and successional change can all reorganize the ecosystem.

♻️ Nutrient cycling

Carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus move between living and non-living components. Decomposers return nutrients to soil for producers to reuse.

⚡ Energy flow

Solar energy is captured by producers and moves through trophic levels. Unlike nutrients, energy is not recycled; much is dissipated as heat.

🫧 Ecosystem services

Ecosystems support air and water purification, climate regulation, habitat, food, fibre and fuel.

Why do higher trophic levels have less energy?

Energy transformations obey thermodynamic principles. Energy changes form, but transfer is inefficient: a substantial fraction is dissipated as heat during respiration, movement, digestion and maintenance.

What changes as we move upward?

Energy available ↓

Each trophic level receives less usable energy than the level below.

Number of organisms often ↓

Lower energy availability can support fewer organisms at higher levels.

Dependence on producers ↑

Because producers form the energy base, damage to them can affect the entire ecosystem.

How does an ecosystem rebuild itself over time?

Ecological succession is the gradual change in community composition over time as organisms modify conditions and new species become established.

Primary succession begins on a surface where soil is initially absent, such as newly exposed rock after lava flow or glacial retreat.

Primary vs secondary succession

Primary

Starts where soil is absent. Pioneer organisms help create and deepen soil, so recovery is usually slower.

Secondary

Follows disturbance where soil remains, such as after many fires, floods or abandoned farmland. Existing soil and biological remnants allow faster recovery.
