05A · FROM SINGLE GATES TO CIRCUITS

Click the inputs and watch signals move through more than one gate.

A circuit is not just a picture of boxes. The output of one gate becomes the input of the next. The interactive examples below let you change A, B and C and immediately see the intermediate value and final output.

1. First see real AND and OR switching

2. Interactive two-stage circuit: Y = AB + C

Here A and B first enter an AND gate. Its output is an intermediate signal X = AB. Then X and C enter an OR gate. Click the three inputs and follow the green signal from left to right.

Stage 1: X = A·B    →    Stage 2: Y = X + C    →    Y = AB + C

Step 1
A and B are read by the AND gate.
Step 2
AND creates intermediate value X = 0.
Step 3
OR combines X with C and produces Y = 0.
Important: The second gate does not directly receive A and B. It receives the result produced by the first gate. That is what it means to connect gates into a circuit.

3. Try the reverse structure: Y = (A + B)C

Now A and B first go through OR. The intermediate output X = A + B then goes into AND together with C. This demonstrates why brackets in Boolean expressions matter.

4. From gates to complete digital systems

Once many gates are connected, they form larger functional blocks. XOR and AND form a half adder. Several gates form a full adder. Multiplexers select data paths, decoders select outputs, and flip-flops add memory.
